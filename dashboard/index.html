How the model works

Coverage. The board carries every protocol the snapshot records with no token at all, put through the field guide's own watchlist rule with its TVL floor removed: no exchange, no bridge or staking category, no versioned pool, no wrapped, staked or staking product, and no exchange's own name at the front. That rule gives protocols on this snapshot and covers all watchlist slugs — both figures are read from the board itself, so this paragraph cannot drift from it. The floor is removed on purpose: a protocol below the snapshot's floor is as good a subject as one above it, and the watchlist is a floor, never a ceiling. The board never ranks an exchange or a representation of an asset that already trades, because those are not protocols waiting on a launch of their own — and the rule that says so is the same rule the board is built with, so this paragraph and the board cannot disagree.

The size curve. A flat multiple prices a $2.7B protocol and a $5M protocol in the same category at the same market-cap-per-TVL. The market does not: the multiple falls as TVL rises. So the multiple is read off a log-log curve fitted to the snapshot's own traded protocols. The anchors are the traded protocols that are not exchanges, with at least $1M of market cap and a market-cap-per-TVL between 0.001 and 10, which drops the dust and the extreme ratios that would otherwise drag the fit. On this snapshot that is . One pooled slope is fitted over every anchor; the intercept is the category's own median residual where the category has at least three anchors, and the global intercept otherwise. The multiple is exp(intercept) × TVL^(slope − 1), clamped to 0.05–1.50 so one outlier cannot set a price. Implied value is then TVL × multiple × growth × maturity × breadth: growth is the 7-day TVL change clamped to −80%…+200% with weight 0.65, maturity rises from 0.60 at listing to 1.00 after three years, and breadth adds 2% per chain up to five. No protocol name is special-cased.

The out-of-sample check, and what it admits. The curve is fitted and scored five times: the anchors are split into five folds by slug order, and each fold is predicted by a curve fitted on the other four. The flat median — the model radar-2 used — is fitted and scored on the very same folds, so the two numbers are like for like. Both numbers are reported side by side and neither is flattered: this is a multiple read off TVL alone, and neither model is a forecast of a token's market cap. The curve is used for its shape — a small protocol prices at a higher multiple of its TVL than a large one, and a flat multiple says the opposite — not for a precision it does not have. A reader should treat every implied value on this page as an order-of-magnitude estimate, and the verdict thresholds are set to survive that: Farm needs five times the gas, not one.

The farm verdict. Every row is netted, not just described. The one assumption, stated as an assumption: 5% of the implied launch value is airdropped and shared equally by 10,000 farmers, so the expected value is the implied value times 0.000005. The gas is the sum of what that row's own plan names — a fresh wallet at $3, the deposit's gas at $8 or $4, a second action at $4 or $2, and $2 a week for the hold — and the deposit is capital that comes back, so it is not a cost. Farm needs the expected value to reach five times the gas, a margin that covers the model's own out-of-sample error; Thin is anything above the gas; Skip is below it. The ninth step of every farm plan states the arithmetic, so a reader can check the call instead of trusting it. The verdict counts on this board: . Net $/hour divides the net by the plan's own hours, its setup steps once and its weekly checks for the hold.
